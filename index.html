      How it works

      The system design and the talk track for this demo. ✅ = checked in a primary source (MetaMask docs, zerohash docs, or seen live in this demo). [verify] = not confirmed.

      System design: what happens when you click Connect

      Everything runs in the browser. There is no backend, and nothing is stored.

      Latency: how long the read took

      Connect a wallet or add an address, and this fills in with real timings from that scan.

      Not measured yet. Each bar is one chain: a block-number lookup, then all its balances at once. Times depend on your connection and free public nodes. Separate timing test, 7 Nov, desktop: 7 requests in parallel took 130–137 ms over 5 runs; 6 returned a balance, and the free node refused one Solana token lookup, so this page reads that balance a different way.

      “Reading is instant. Moving needs approvals, confirmations and sometimes a hold, so that’s the part our system has to make async.”

      Talk track

      “I connect my own self-custody wallet. The page learns my public addresses, reads every balance straight from the blockchains, and lays them out the way a brokerage app could, ready to move.”

      1. Connect: MetaMask gives us addresses, nothing else

      1. “When the page loads, it asks the browser which wallets are installed.”

        MetaMask announces itself twice: as an Ethereum-style wallet, and as a Solana and Bitcoin wallet.

        EIP-6963 (EVM) · Wallet Standard (Solana, Bitcoin) ✅

      2. “I click Connect MetaMask.”

        The page asks MetaMask for account addresses only. First time on a site, MetaMask asks me to unlock and approve.

        eth_requestAccounts · standard:connect · bitcoin:connect ✅

      3. “MetaMask hands back three public addresses: one Ethereum-style, one Solana, one Bitcoin.”

        That’s all it returns. No keys, no balances, no permission to move anything.

      If there’s no popup, say: “MetaMask remembers I approved this site, so it shares the addresses without asking again. Sending would still need my approval.” (Seen live: an approved site gets addresses even while MetaMask is locked ✅ observed; not documented [verify].)

      2. Read the chains: public data, no wallet involved

      1. “Now the page asks each blockchain: what does this address hold?”

        One Ethereum-style address is checked on 6 chains: Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, OP, Polygon, Avalanche. Token contract addresses come from the issuers, such as Circle for USDC ✅.

        JSON-RPC eth_getBalance (ETH, POL, AVAX) · eth_call → balanceOf(address) on each token contract (USDC, USDT, WETH, WBTC…)

      2. Solana address.

        getBalance (SOL) · getAccountInfo on the wallet’s USDC and USDT token accounts ✅

      3. Bitcoin address.

        mempool.space GET /address/{address}, Blockstream as fallback ✅

      4. “Prices come from CoinGecko. Only coin names are sent, never my address.”

        CoinGecko GET /simple/price ✅

      5. “Eight chains in under a second. Each line shows the block number it read, so this is live, not cached.”

        Tiles for BTC, ETH, SOL and USDC, the full table, and the step list.

      “Anyone can read these balances from an address; any block explorer shows the same. The wallet’s only job here was to tell us which addresses are mine.”

      Fixed vs live: the list of chains, public nodes and token contracts is a config file. The addresses, balances, block numbers and prices are fetched live on every click.

      3. The brokerage view

      1. “Here’s how a brokerage app could show this.” (📱 Open in brokerage app)

        Every holding found, with checkbox, amount and USD value, already filled in from the read above. Wrapped tokens (WETH, WBTC) are shown separately.

      2. “I select everything and tap Review.”

        One transfer per asset: from my address, to a deposit address the brokerage gives me, and which coin pays the network fee. It warns if a chain has no coin for the fee.

      3. “The prototype stops here, on purpose. It never asks the wallet to send.”

        “Approve in MetaMask” is disabled.

      4. What would happen next in production (say it, it isn’t built)

      1. The brokerage gets my deposit addresses from zerohash.

        One per customer per asset; on EVM chains, one forwarding address per network that takes the native coin and tokens alike ✅.

        POST /deposits/digital_asset_addresses ✅

      2. The app asks MetaMask to send, pre-filled with destination, coin and amount. I can only confirm or reject.

        EVM: one bundle per chain, one confirmation; it may first ask to upgrade to a MetaMask smart account ✅. Solana: one transaction can carry several transfers ✅. Bitcoin: one send from the wallet [verify the exact MetaMask method].

        wallet_sendCalls (EIP-5792)

      3. I approve once per chain.

        MetaMask signs with my key, which never leaves MetaMask, and broadcasts.

      4. zerohash credits after 1 confirmation for BTC, ETH and SOL ✅.

        Typical times: BTC about 10 min, ETH 12–30 s, SOL seconds.

      5. Then compliance.

        Screening, and for self-custody deposits the Travel Rule needs the sending wallet owner’s information; unverified deposits are held ✅.

      6. Status comes back by webhook.

        deposit.status_changed → completed ✅

      “Today, an AUTH deposit can’t be pre-filled (no setting fixes asset, network or amount ✅) and there’s no batch or multi-asset deposit ✅. With what we just read, the brokerage could pre-fill every transfer and cut it to one approval per chain, not one per asset.”

      Likely questions

      “Can you move my money with this?”
      No. Connecting only shares public addresses. Every send needs the owner’s approval in MetaMask, and this page can’t even ask for one.
      “Is this real data?”
      Yes. Live calls to public nodes; the block numbers prove it. Nothing is stored.
      “Why not one approval for everything?”
      Each chain is a separate network, so the floor is one approval per chain. No provider found delivers several assets as-is in one approval; Mesh SmartFunding combines sources but converts them into one asset ✅.
      “What’s not covered?”
      Tokens outside the list, NFTs, DeFi positions; Bitcoin is read for the one shared address only.
      Covers native coins plus 21 tokens on Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, OP Mainnet, Polygon and Avalanche; SOL, USDC and USDT on Solana; and BTC. It doesn’t cover other tokens, NFTs or DeFi positions. Bitcoin is read per shared address, so funds on other addresses of the same wallet may be missed. Prices from CoinGecko. A personal prototype.
